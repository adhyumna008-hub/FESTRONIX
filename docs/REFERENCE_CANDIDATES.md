## Pulse

### P3 — UX London — strongest balanced Pulse reference

- **URL:** [2026 site](https://2026.uxlondon.com/); [speaker grid](https://2026.uxlondon.com/speakers/).
- **Type / classification:** Live professional design event; Pulse.
- **Why it fits:** Large readable sans typography, deep blue with restrained cyan/blue graphic accents, clear dates/navigation and friendly but mature presentation. The speaker grid has consistent portraits, names, roles and topic pills. This provides both identity and real content presentation, without a SaaS sales-page structure.
- **Borrow conceptually:** A concise information ladder; a small palette; playful geometry outside text; consistent card metadata and tag order; varied but orderly community-photo sections. Translate speaker grouping into FESTRONIX event grouping rather than duplicating the content.
- **Do not copy:** Logo, circular portraits, exact blue palette, graphic shapes, marketing copy or people/photos. Avoid a long headline consuming the entire first viewport.
- **Saved:** `references/pulse/ux-london-desktop.jpg`, `ux-london-cards.jpg` (community photo composition), `ux-london-speakers.jpg` (actual grid).
- **Manual capture priority:** Mobile hero and event/schedule presentation. Existing desktop grid is usable.

### P4 — Hatch Conference — strongest hierarchy and schedule reference

- **URL:** [Home](https://www.hatchconference.com/); [agenda](https://www.hatchconference.com/agenda).
- **Type / classification:** Live design conference; Pulse.
- **Why it fits:** Oversized fine sans lettering, quiet white foundation, orange action accent and generous space create premium clarity. Dates and event format are easy to identify. Agenda text exposes time, stage, session, speaker and duration grouping.
- **Borrow conceptually:** Strong display/body scale contrast, visible date/location, one high-priority action, restrained accent and a predictable schedule metadata hierarchy.
- **Do not copy:** Its invitation-only model, sponsor set, enormous cropped AGENDA lettering, exact pill navigation or branding. The target audience is senior professionals; FESTRONIX needs warmer student-facing language.
- **Saved:** `references/pulse/hatch-desktop.jpg`, `hatch-agenda.jpg`.
- **Manual capture priority:** Fully settled agenda cards; current screenshot has faded content and is not a final card-style specification. Capture mobile too.

## Panel

### PNL1 — Awwwards Conference Tokyo 2020 / SHIFTBRAIN — strongest independent manga conference reference

- **URL:** [Designer case study](https://shiftbrain.com/work/awwwards_2020/?lang=en).
- **Type / classification:** Professional case study of a historical conference website; Panel. Not an anime fan site.
- **Why it fits:** The designers explicitly connect its conference identity to manga and illustrated real city scenes. Visible artwork combines warm red/purple tones, ink-like outlines, subtle printed texture, dynamic city perspective and expressive white script with dark edging. It demonstrates an adult professional conference using illustration rather than stock comic-template motifs.
- **Borrow conceptually:** Illustrate original campus/technology scenes from real environments; use a controlled ink palette and cinematic perspective; pair one expressive display treatment with plain readable navigation and body copy. Place graphic panels around content, not through it.
- **Do not copy:** Tokyo cityscape, Japanese cultural shorthand, lettering, color recipe or illustrated scenes. Designer-described transitions are provenance, not verified behavior or a required implementation.
- **Saved:** `references/panel/awwwards-tokyo-case.jpg`, `awwwards-tokyo-art.jpg`.
- **Manual capture priority:** The case study's deeper speaker/content panels and responsive mockups. Current captures show the top and a partial hero artwork.

### PNL2 — beyond tellerrand — strongest live printed/editorial identity

- **URL:** [Conference](https://beyondtellerrand.com/).
- **Type / classification:** Live coding/creativity conference; Panel, with secondary Pulse hierarchy value.
- **Why it fits:** Giant condensed display lettering, collage-like colored patterns and printed grain create mature editorial impact. A separate cream information panel gives dates/location a quiet reading surface. It connects expressive festival identity to clear event details.
- **Borrow conceptually:** Expressive masthead plus calm metadata panel; limited print-texture zones; strong display/body contrast; predictable navigation outside artwork.
- **Do not copy:** Letter masks, patterned collage, cardboard-box mark, pink/cyan composition or ticket panel layout. Do not bake edition/year into the core FESTRONIX logo.
- **Saved:** `references/panel/beyond-tellerrand-desktop.jpg`.
- **Manual capture priority:** Full hero CTA, event/content panels and mobile. Current desktop viewport crops the bottom of the information panel.

### PNL3 — Vulture Festival / Andy Outis — strongest comic texture and graphic label system

- **URL:** [Designer case study](https://www.andyoutis.com/and-more/case-study-vulture-festival-branding).
- **Type / classification:** Professional historical identity/website case study; Panel. Focus on early comic/print treatments; later editions use different styles.
- **Why it fits:** The designer documents comic-influenced bursts, word bubbles, sunbursts and dot patterns, followed by a website redesign with desktop/mobile examples. The captured artwork has clear large titles and event metadata within a coordinated print vocabulary. This is a repeatable editorial language rather than a cartoon mascot template.
- **Borrow conceptually:** Limited graphic label shapes, texture scales that support hierarchy, poster-like event promotion and an identity system that extends across public pages. Keep long copy on quiet surfaces.
- **Do not copy:** Vulture mark, celebrity imagery, sunburst pattern, exact cyan/yellow palette, event copy or ticket-sales-only priorities. Do not combine every historical edition into one FESTRONIX theme.
- **Saved:** `references/panel/vulture-case.jpg` (early promotional artwork, not the website itself).
- **Manual capture priority:** Case-study section titled Website Redesign, including desktop and mobile examples. Current capture supports graphic language; it does not establish web card anatomy.

### PNL4 — Presstival / Eclypsys and Monokini — strongest sophisticated editorial restraint

- **URL:** [Creator case study](https://eclypsys.ch/en/projets/presstival).
- **Type / classification:** Professional journalism-festival website case study; Panel's editorial side, not manga evidence by itself.
- **Why it fits:** Its embedded website image shows forceful headline blocks, black graphic labels, a vivid violet/coral poster field and a quieter white text section. This is useful evidence that a graphic festival identity can retain readable editorial content. The creator credits Monokini's typography work.
- **Borrow conceptually:** Poster-like date/place labels, disciplined black/white framing, expressive hero followed by a quiet reading panel, and a small set of coherent text styles.
- **Do not copy:** Identity, exact poster imagery, color combination, journalistic slogans or agency-site chrome. CMS/plugin and performance claims in the case study supply no FESTRONIX architecture or verified performance conclusions.
- **Saved:** `references/panel/presstival-case.jpg`, `presstival-art.jpg` (embedded mockup, partial).
- **Manual capture priority:** Full-resolution embedded mockup and live program/mobile views if available. Current evidence is smaller than a direct website screenshot.
